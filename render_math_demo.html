💡 Calculus & Topology Explanation
Radial Waves & Gradient Vector Fields

This surface visualizes a damped sinusoidal wave propagating radially outward from the origin in $\mathbb{R}^2$:

z(r) = 2.8 · sin(1.1 r) · e^(-0.07 r) | r = √(x² + y²)
  • Radial Symmetry: Because $z$ depends only on $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, horizontal cross-sections ($z = c$) form perfect concentric circles.
  • Gradient Vector ($\nabla z$): The gradient $\nabla z = \frac{dz}{dr} \hat{r}$ points strictly along the radial direction, perpendicular to contour lines.
  • Critical Points ($\nabla z = 0$): Stationary ring circles occur whenever $\frac{dz}{dr} = 0$, creating alternating crest rings and trough valleys.
  • Exponential Damping ($e^{-0.07r}$): Models physical energy dissipation as wavefronts expand outward across the plane.
